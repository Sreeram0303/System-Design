Chapter 2 — Back-of-the-Envelope Estimation

Three building blocks — powers of two, latency numbers, and availability numbers — turn a vague "how would this scale?" into a defensible number in under a minute.

"Back-of-the-envelope calculations are estimates you create using a combination of thought experiments and common performance numbers to get a good feel for which designs will meet your requirements." Jeff Dean, Google Senior Fellow

01 Power of two

Data volume in distributed systems always reduces to bytes, so powers of two are worth having memorized cold. A byte is 8 bits; one ASCII character is 1 byte.

PowerApprox valueBytes
2⁷128—
2⁸256—
2¹⁰1 thousand1 KB
2¹⁶—64 KB
2²⁰1 million1 MB
2³⁰1 billion1 GB
2³²—4 GB
2⁴⁰1 trillion1 TB

Bar length is on a log scale — the gap between the fastest and slowest row here is roughly 300 million×, not visible at all on a linear scale.

Takeaways
  • Memory is fast, disk is slow.
  • Avoid disk seeks where possible.
  • Simple compression algorithms are fast — compress before sending over the network.
  • Data centers sit in different regions; moving data between them takes real, measurable time.

03 Availability numbers

High availability — a system stays continuously operational for a long stretch, measured as a percentage. Most real services sit between 99% and 100%.

SLA (service level agreement) — the formal contract between provider and customer defining guaranteed uptime. AWS, GCP, and Azure all commit to 99.9%+ SLAs.

Uptime is discussed in "nines" — more nines, less downtime:

AvailabilityDowntime / yearDowntime / monthDowntime / day
99% (two nines)~3.65 days~7.2 hrs~14.4 min
99.9% (three nines)~8.76 hrs~43.8 min~1.44 min
99.99% (four nines)~52.6 min~4.38 min~8.64 sec
99.999% (five nines)~5.26 min~25.9 sec~864 ms

04 Worked example — Twitter QPS & storage

Illustrative numbers only — not Twitter's real figures.

Assumptions
  • 300 million monthly active users (MAU)
  • 50% of users are active daily
  • Users post 2 tweets/day on average
  • 10% of tweets contain media
  • Data retained for 5 years

QPS estimate

  1. DAU = 300M MAU × 50%= 150 million
  2. Tweet QPS = 150M × 2 tweets ÷ 24h ÷ 3600s≈ 3,500 QPS
  3. Peak QPS = 2 × average QPS≈ 7,000 QPS

Media storage estimate

  1. Avg tweet: tweet_id 64 B + text 140 B + media 1 MB≈ 1 MB / tweet w/ media
  2. Daily media = 150M × 2 tweets × 10% × 1 MB= 30 TB / day
  3. 5-year media = 30 TB × 365 × 5≈ 55 PB

05 Interview tips

  • ✓ Round and approximate — 99987 / 9.1 → just do 100,000 / 10
  • ✓ Write down your assumptions so they're referenceable
  • ✓ Label your units — "5" is ambiguous, "5 MB" isn't
  • ✓ Rehearse the common ones: QPS, peak QPS, storage, cache size, server count
